## Definitions and assumptions

- **Walk segment j:** Initiator rows strictly between the end of the dwell lap at d_from and the end of the following walk lap (the start of the dwell at d_to). Nine walks per run, 300 -> 30 m; the walk-in below 30 m is excluded.
- **Interpolated truth:** d_true(t) = d_from + (d_to - d_from) * (t - t_start) / (t_end - t_start). **Assumption: constant walking speed within each walk.** Every residual, MAE, IQR, slope ratio and lag below depends on it.
- **Residual:** reading minus d_true, SUCCESS rows. Median residual, MAE = mean |residual|, residual IQR (linear interpolation, as for the dwell IQRs).
- **Offset-corrected MAE:** mean |residual minus the static-tier mean signed error of the bandwidth|.
- **Slope ratio:** least-squares slope of reading vs time over the segment, divided by the true speed (-30 m / walk duration).
- **Lag:** time shift tau from -3.0 to +3.0 s in 0.1 s steps, reading(t) against d_true(t - tau); positive tau = readings late. Rows within 10 s either side of the walk are used, with the truth held at the marker distance outside the walk, because inside a constant-speed ramp a time shift is indistinguishable from a constant range offset. Criterion A minimises mean |residual|; criterion B minimises mean |residual minus its median| (removes a constant bias). The estimate is driven by the two walk ends, where the constant-speed assumption is weakest.
- **Moving vs stationary:** each walk against the mean of its two end-marker dwells in the same run (dwell windows after the 5 s warm-up discard of Methodology 7.2; dwell MAE and corrected MAE use the row-level definition above with the marker as truth). Averaging over the nine walks counts the 300 m and 30 m dwells once and every other dwell twice, so the pooled dwell values differ from the per-run aggregates in `aggregates.md`.
- **Stopwatch offset:** file time = lap time + offset. Baseline 0 s as in the paper; +0.2 and +1.1 s are the two best fits, -0.2 and -1.1 s cover the unrecorded sign convention. The offset moves walk and dwell windows together.
- **Bootstrap:** 10,000 resamples of the nine walks, `random.Random(20261105)` (reseeded per sensitivity row), 95 % percentile intervals; the bandwidth difference pairs the same walks at both bandwidths.
- **Few-row flag:** a segment with fewer than 30 rows after trimming (an analysis threshold, not a protocol rule).

## Motion onset and offset delays (supplementary)

Premise (operator, 2026-11-05): the lap log marks the true start and end of each walk, so t_start and t_end are taken as the true motion times. For each walk the reading is fitted with a truth held at d_from until t1, falling at constant speed to d_to at t2, then held, plus a free constant; criterion B of the lag search. **Onset delay** = t1 - t_start; **offset delay** = t2 - t_end (positive = reading still changing after the walk ended). Search: onset -10.0 to +20.0 s, offset -20.0 to +20.0 s, 0.5 s steps, at least 10 s of motion, rows within 25 s either side of the walk, offset 0 s. A fit on a search bound is censored (marked `c`). Start-overshoot rows (the rows behind the start excursions of the excursion check: 3-row rolling median more than 5 m above the level of the dwell just left, within 10 s of t_start) are left out of the fit at both bandwidths; the unmasked onset is listed for comparison. Under a pure reading lag both delays equal the lag; under a walking speed that is slower at the ends than in the middle the onset is positive and the offset negative. Hence **lag component** = (onset + offset) / 2 and **speed-profile component** = (onset - offset) / 2. A stopwatch offset moves both delays by the same amount, so it moves the lag component one for one and leaves the speed-profile component unchanged.

## Findings (observations only)

1. **Coverage.** 9 walks per run, 40 to 63 rows per walk at 406.25 kHz and 43 to 60 at 1625 kHz; 0 non-SUCCESS rows and 0 negative readings across all 18 walks. With the 4 s trim the smallest walk keeps 28 rows.
2. **The constant-speed truth does not match the readings.** The fitted slope of reading vs time exceeds the true mean walking speed in 8 of 9 walks at 406.25 kHz and 9 of 9 at 1625 kHz (mean ratio 1.24 and 1.18), while the stationary per-marker medians scale with distance at slopes of 1.004 to 1.016. Taking the lap boundaries as the true walk start and end, the onset delay is +1.4 s [+0.1, +2.6] and the offset delay -5.8 s [-8.6, -3.6] at 1625 kHz (the reading stops changing before the walk ends); at 406.25 kHz they are +3.9 s [-0.1, +7.0] and -1.7 s [-7.1, +4.7] (finding 9). At 1625 kHz the walk-end excursions exceed 5 m at 1 of 18 walk ends, so the motion fit and the slope ratio there are not driven by them; at 406.25 kHz they are confounded with the excursions of finding 8.
3. **Bandwidth ordering while walking.** MAE against the interpolated truth is 13.0 m at 406.25 kHz and 3.9 m at 1625 kHz, paired difference +9.1 m [+7.9, +10.3]. The sign holds and the interval excludes 0 in 15 of 15 sensitivity rows (difference +7.1 to +10.7 m).
4. **Most of that difference is the fixed static bias.** After subtracting the static-tier mean signed error, the paired MAE difference is +1.3 m [+0.2, +2.5]. **Its interval includes 0 in 8 of 15 sensitivity rows and its sign flips in 1** (range -0.2 to +2.2 m).
5. **Spread while walking.** The residual IQR is larger at 406.25 kHz than at 1625 kHz by +2.6 m [+0.7, +4.8] (interval excludes 0 in 15 of 15 rows), and larger while walking than at the end-marker dwells at both bandwidths (+2.9 and +3.0 m; all 15 rows). Part of this excess is the truth model: on moving rows only, with fitted timing, the walk-minus-dwell IQR is +1.1 m [+0.0, +2.4] at 406.25 kHz and +1.8 m [+1.1, +2.6] at 1625 kHz.
6. **Signed error while walking vs stationary.** At 1625 kHz the walk median residual is -2.2 m [-3.5, -1.0] relative to the end dwells against the constant-speed interpolated truth (interval includes 0 in 3 of 15 rows), and +0.3 m [-0.1, +0.7] on moving rows against the fitted motion timing. At 406.25 kHz it is +0.8 m [-0.4, +2.1] and **its sign flips in 3 of 15 rows** (all at offset +1.1 s).
7. **Lag.** The interpolation-based lag search is not informative: criterion A sits on the +/-3 s bound in 9 of 9 walks at 406.25 kHz and criterion B in 3 and 4 of 9 walks. The onset/offset decomposition gives a lag component of -2.2 s [-3.8, -0.7] at 1625 kHz (offset 0 s; -3.3 s [-4.8, -1.8] at offset +1.1 s), below zero, which a reading delay cannot produce. In the 5 s after the walk ends the 1625 kHz reading changes in 1 of 9 walks (mean slope +0.01 m/s [-0.21, +0.21], median interval half-width 0.63 m/s). At 406.25 kHz the lag component is +1.1 s [-1.1, +3.1] and the reading changes after the walk end in 0 of 9 walks, but the median interval half-width there is 2.26 m/s, wider than the walking speed, so that check cannot detect a change of walking size. A stopwatch offset moves the lag component one for one, so a lag no larger than the offset uncertainty (up to +1.1 s) is not separable from it.
8. **Excursions at the walk ends, 406.25 kHz.** The 3-row rolling median rises more than 5 m above the level of the dwell just left in 3 of 9 walk starts (largest +11.6 m) and falls more than 5 m below the level of the dwell just reached in 4 of 9 walk ends (largest -11.4 m). At 1625 kHz the counts are 1 and 0. Both directions steepen the fitted slope of a walk.
9. **Classification (rule of the 2026-11-05 brief).** 1625 kHz: **non-uniform walking speed (ground-truth model)** under both fit searches; 8 of 9 walks have onset > 0 and offset < 0, none is censored, and the speed-profile component is +3.6 s [+2.5, +5.0]. 406.25 kHz: **censored (1 censored fit(s) change the class)** under the main search and **mixed (1 censored fit(s); same class without them)** under the narrower one. Walk 4 (210 -> 180 m) is censored in both: its end dwells differ by 20.0 m in reading against 30 m of walk. Leaving the start-overshoot rows out of the fit moves the mean onset by +0.0 s at 406.25 kHz and -0.1 s at 1625 kHz.
